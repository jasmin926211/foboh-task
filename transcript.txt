│ decision fully auditable at a glance.                                        │

│ 2. Add marginInsight — flags when the winning price diverges >10% from       │
│ same-tier average (alerts suppliers to unexpected pricing gaps)              │
│ 3. Keep all existing fields (newPrice, appliedProfile, tier, floor fields)   │

  ---
  Verdict: The waterfall approach is better

  Three key reasons:

  1. Auditability — Each entry is self-documenting. A supplier (or support
  engineer) can look at the response and immediately understand why a specific
  profile won or lost without comparing fields across entries. This is the core
  pattern used by Salesforce CPQ, Oracle Pricing, and similar enterprise tools.
  2. Single source of truth — Two separate arrays (candidateProfiles /
  rejectedProfiles) force every consumer (frontend, reporting, integrations) to
  merge and correlate them. The waterfall eliminates that — one array, one sort
  order, one rendering loop.
  3. Proactive alerting — marginInsight adds value the old approach simply
  didn't have. Flagging when the winning price diverges significantly from peers
   catches misconfigured profiles before they impact revenue.

  The only trade-off is slightly more computation on the backend (generating
  reason strings, computing margin averages), but this is negligible compared to
   the DB queries already being made.
